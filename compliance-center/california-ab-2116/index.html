California AB 2116: Preliminary Readiness Checklist

California will license commercial financing providers and brokers starting in 2028. If you fund California small businesses, you are likely a commercial financing provider under AB 2116. Here is what you can prepare now.

Last reviewed October 12, 2026 · Preliminary · Not legal advice
Preliminary. The DFPI has not yet proposed regulations for AB 2116. Proposed rules will go through a public comment period before they are final, and the application itself has not been published. This checklist is meant to help you get organized early. It will be updated as guidance develops.

Key Dates

  1. Sept. 30, 2026Signed into lawChapter 823, Statutes of 2026.
  2. 2027 (expected)DFPI rulemakingThe DFPI is expected to propose implementing regulations, which go through a public comment period before they are final.
  3. Jan. 1, 2028Conduct rules beginConfession of judgment ban, UDAAP rules, broker APR posting, recordkeeping. New agreements must meet the enforceability test.
  4. July 1, 2028License requiredProviders and brokers must be licensed. Those with a complete application on file by this date may keep operating while it is pending.
  5. Mar. 15, 2029First annual reportProviders report 2028 transaction counts, dollar volume, and APR data.

Purpose
Business
not personal, family, or household

Products. Accounts receivable purchases and factoring, asset-based lending (including merchant cash advances, as commentators read it), commercial loans, commercial open-end credit, and lease financing.

Brokers. You are a commercial financing broker if you do any of the following in connection with a provider’s financing:

  1. Transmitting a prospective recipient’s sensitive data to a provider for compensation tied to a referral
  2. Referring recipients under an agreement that they meet criteria involving sensitive data
  3. Participating in negotiations between a provider and a recipient
  4. Advising or recommending a transaction based on the recipient’s sensitive data
  5. Participating in preparing financing documents, including applications
  6. Communicating a provider’s approval decision to the recipient
  7. Charging the recipient a fee for application-related services

Exempt. Banks and other federally regulated lenders, transactions secured by real property, certain vehicle dealer and rental company financing, and very low-volume activity (one or fewer transactions in 12 months, or five or fewer incidental transactions).

Still Open

Questions we expect the DFPI or the legislature to address:

  • The six-month gap. The enforceability rule applies to agreements from January 1, 2028, but licensing is not required until July 1, 2028. Commentators expect a fix or DFPI guidance.
  • Existing CFL licensees. Whether current finance lender and broker licensees need to amend their licenses to add the new categories, and how.
  • The application. Net worth, bond, and other requirements for the new provider and broker categories.
  • APR posting. How brokers should calculate and present average and maximum APR on their websites.
  • Ability to repay. What the DFPI will treat as adequate consideration of a recipient’s ability to repay.

We will update this page as regulations are proposed. Members will hear from us directly.
